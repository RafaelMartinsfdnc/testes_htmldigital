Throughout its history, Fundação Dorina Nowill para Cegos has built a reputation for excellence in service delivery and a steadfast commitment to the inclusion of visually impaired people. In 2025, this commitment was also reflected in the expansion of the Foundation’s physical facilities, an important milestone that supports the continued growth of its activities and provides even better conditions for serving clients and their families.

Investing in new facilities and modernizing infrastructure are part of the Foundation’s ongoing commitment to maintaining excellence in its work. More than a physical expansion, these improvements reflect its responsibility to preserve the quality of its services and ensure that the institution is prepared to meet the needs of an ever-growing number of clients without losing the individualized care that has always defined its work.

With nearly eight decades of history, the Foundation continues to share knowledge, build partnerships, and expand its reach across different regions of Brazil. More than a record of its activities, this report is also an opportunity to recognize and thank everyone who contributes to the Foundation’s continued growth with quality, responsibility, and purpose.

Organizational Profile

 

SUBTITLE: Photo: Fundação Dorina headquarters. END OF SUBTITLE.

 

Fundação Dorina Nowill para Cegos works to promote the autonomy and independence of visually impaired people, while also raising awareness of the importance of inclusion, accessibility, and every person’s right to participate fully in social, cultural, and professional life.

The Foundation was established through the initiative of Dorina Nowill, an educator who lost her sight at a young age and chose to transform her personal experience into a lifelong mission. After facing barriers to continuing her education and accessing reading materials, Dorina launched a movement to expand the production of braille books in Brazil. This effort led to the creation of the Foundation for the Book for the Blind in Brazil (Fundação para o Livro do Cego no Brasil) in 1946, with the goal of ensuring that people with visual impairments had access to information and education. The production of accessible books, which marked the beginning of this journey, remains one of the cornerstones of the Foundation’s work.

Over time, the Foundation expanded its scope of work by developing new areas of activity, always focused on promoting autonomy, access to knowledge, and social inclusion. The organization modernized its operations, developed technologies and solutions in the publishing, printing, and audiovisual fields, and established specialized services for habilitation, rehabilitation, and family support, evolving alongside changes in society and the needs of the people it serves.

Today, recognized in Brazil and internationally for the quality of its work, Fundação Dorina Nowill para Cegos continues to expand its projects and partnerships while remaining true to the commitment that inspired its creation: to help ensure that an increasing number of people with visual impairments have access to opportunities, independence, and full participation in every sphere of society.

Visual Impairment in Brazil

 

SUBTITLE: Photo: Alice Vitória Domingues, served by Fundação Dorina. END OF SUBTITLE.

 

In Brazil, data from the Brazilian Institute of Geography and Statistics (IBGE) indicate that approximately 6.5 million people have a visual impairment, including around 528,000 people who are blind and more than 6 million who have severe difficulty seeing, a condition characterized as low vision. These figures underscore the need for public policies, specialized services, and initiatives aimed at promoting accessibility, rehabilitation, and greater autonomy for people with visual impairments.

According to the World Health Organization (WHO), between 60% and 80% of cases of blindness could be prevented through early diagnosis and appropriate treatment.

Chair of the Board of Trustees

 

 

The year 2025 was one of great significance for Fundação Dorina Nowill para Cegos. Habilitation and Rehabilitation

 

SUBTITLE: Photo: Visit to the Fire Department. END OF SUBTITLE.

 

For many visually impaired people, the Habilitation and Rehabilitation area is their primary point of entry into Fundação Dorina Nowill para Cegos. It is through these services that the process of developing autonomy, independence, and inclusion begins, involving not only the individuals served but also their families.

In 2025, Fundação Dorina reached another important milestone, providing more than 55,000 services throughout the year. Notably, the number of active children exceeded the number of young people served, reflecting the strengthening of the Foundation’s outreach initiatives and its investment in the early identification of visual impairments. Early diagnosis leads to better habilitation outcomes, fostering significant gains in child development while better preparing families to provide care and support.

With this focus, the area expanded its activities for children, adolescents, and their families, offering workshops and programs in the expressive, cultural, and educational fields, including art, judo, mini goalball, tai chi chuan, choir, theater, braille literacy, and braille transcription training for family members.

External activities also expanded in 2025, building on initiatives launched the previous year. These included inclusive rowing, promoted by the Brazilian Council of Ophthalmology, inclusive sailing in partnership with the Sailing Sense Project, and cultural activities at museums, movie theaters, theaters, Sesc cultural centers, and other venues. These experiences help strengthen autonomy, social interaction, and the right to inclusive leisure, enabling clients to apply the skills developed during their rehabilitation services while also providing the technical team with valuable opportunities for observation and assessment.

Internally, the team was strengthened through the recruitment of new professionals, bringing fresh perspectives and expanding the Foundation’s capacity to serve. In 2025, music therapy became firmly established as an important tool for fostering participation and a sense of belonging among the people served.

Takkyu Volley also remained an important part of the Foundation’s activities in 2025.

 

Page 17

 

As the first sport introduced at Fundação Dorina, it combines elements of volleyball and table tennis and promotes inclusion by enabling people of different ages, with or without disabilities, to participate together.

Another milestone of the year was the opening of the Low Vision Clinic at Fundação Dorina’s Unit 2. Combined with a specialized ophthalmology and orthoptics team, the new facility reduced waiting times for appointments, improved scheduling, and provided a more welcoming environment tailored to clients’ needs.

The Habilitation and Rehabilitation area also shared its expertise with other Brazilian institutions, helping expand services for visually impaired people through important partnerships with the Santa Catarina Association for the Integration of Blind People (ACIC) and the Florivaldo Vargas Mato Grosso do Sul Institute for Blind People (ISMAC).

 

 

Complementary box:

Takkyu Volley: Sport, Autonomy, and New Possibilities

Takkyu Volley is one of the activities offered within the Habilitation and Rehabilitation program. It can be practiced by people of all ages, with or without disabilities, encouraging social interaction and participation.

The activity creates an environment in which participants feel encouraged to explore movements and challenges they may not experience in other settings. For the technical team, the sport also serves as an important space for observation and follow-up, helping to expand support and learning strategies while contributing to the development of autonomy and greater participation for visually impaired people.

Inclusive Reading Network

 

The Inclusive Reading Network works in partnership with organizations in the fields of education, reading, and culture across Brazil, promoting the distribution of accessible books and promoting dialogue on the implementation of accessibility practices in a variety of settings. The Network seeks to expand access to reading for visually impaired people, while also supporting the training of professionals and institutions committed to inclusion.

In 2025, the team participated in events related to books, reading, and accessibility across different regions of Brazil. Highlights included braillevery, held in João Pessoa (Paraíba); the 11th Reading for All Seminar, in Salvador (Bahia); the Reading Suitcase Project (Mala de Leitura), in Agudo (Rio Grande do Sul); the 24th Ribeirão Preto International Book Fair, in São Paulo; and the 4th Federal District Accessibility Fair.

Throughout the year, the Foundation also organized the Inclusive Reading Meetings, a training series held at Fundação Dorina’s headquarters, bringing together partners from different regions of Brazil. As part of this initiative, the Network Invites (Rede Convida) live webinar series featured guest speakers who shared their experiences in producing accessible educational materials, organizing inclusive book clubs, promoting cultural accessibility, and publishing books in multiple accessible formats.

LEGO® Braille Bricks

 

Created in 2020, the LEGO® Braille Bricks program is an initiative developed by Fundação Dorina Nowill para Cegos in partnership with the LEGO Foundation, Denmark, to support inclusive literacy. The educational resource uses specially designed bricks that combine the braille alphabet with printed letters, enabling children with and without visual impairments to learn together in a playful and collaborative way.

Throughout 2025, the program significantly expanded its presence across different regions of Brazil, establishing itself as a widely adopted educational resource. The number of participating educators reached its highest level since the program’s launch, as did the number of partnerships established with Municipal Departments of Education, specialized institutions, and private schools. Teacher training has remained one of the program’s main pillars, enabling the resource to be integrated into everyday classroom practice and reach an ever-growing number of students.

During the year, the program was featured at several events and professional development initiatives, including the 11th World Blindness Summit, where practical demonstrations of LEGO® Braille Bricks and hands-on workshops were conducted with educators and specialists from Brazil and abroad. The 1st Regional LEGO® Braille Bricks Workshop was also held, providing educators who had previously completed the training with an opportunity to deepen their pedagogical practices and exchange experiences.

Another highlight was the 3rd LEGO® Braille Bricks Award, which recognized inclusive teaching practices developed by educators from different regions of Brazil. The finalist initiatives received awards with the support of institutional partners, encouraging the continuation and wider adoption of these classroom experiences.

In 2025, Fundação Dorina also inaugurated the LEGO® Braille Bricks Interactive Room, designed as a reference space for training and demonstrations of the educational resource. The facility was created to promote the sharing of best practices, expand knowledge of the braille literacy process, and strengthen the use of inclusive educational resources in schools.

Commercial

 

In 2025, Fundação Dorina’s commercial strategy focused on strengthening relationships with strategic clients and expanding opportunities in the Braille, Audiovisual, and Digital areas. In the Braille area, efforts were concentrated on consolidating partnerships with educational publishers, resulting in increased production throughout the year for clients such as Arco Educação, Somos Educação, Moderna, and Etapa.

In the Audiovisual area, the Foundation continued its partnerships with Audible and Bookwire, with a particular focus on audiobook production, helping expand the availability of accessible titles in the publishing market. In the Digital area, Fundação Dorina continued to be recognized for producing image descriptions and Digital Educational Objects (DEOs), as well as developing accessible content in EPUB 3 and HTML, in partnership with publishers including SM Educação, Editora do Brasil, and Saber Educação. In 2025, the area reached 59,383 pages produced, based on the original print-page metric, and made 1,437 titles accessible. These figures reflect the continued growth in demand and the Foundation’s expanding role in producing accessible digital content.

The Commercial area also resumed strategic partnerships and created new opportunities through consulting projects, with highlights including initiatives such as Nestlé – Talking Labels (Rótulos que Falam) and VML – Colors You Can Feel (Cores que Tocam). The team also delivered lectures and workshops focused on accessibility awareness, including initiatives developed in partnership with HOYA Lens Brazil.

Throughout the year, the Commercial area exceeded its performance target, delivering results above expectations while expanding the number of projects completed.

Editorial and Printing

 

 

Complementary box:

DIGITAL EDITORIAL

The Digital Editorial area specializes in converting educational, literary, and Other text-based materials into accessible digital formats, such as HTML5 and EPUB 3, ensuring that people with visual impairments can navigate and access their content.

 

BRAILLE EDITORIAL AND PRINTING

The Braille Editorial and Printing areas specialize in the adaptation, proofreading, production, and printing of Braille materials, transforming educational, literary, and informational content into accessible physical formats for people who are blind, with quality, accuracy, and accessibility.

End of complement.

Services provided to educational institutions, publishers, and educational systems continued to expand, supported by the continuation of key partnerships and the addition of new clients throughout the year. The Braille Printing area maintained a strong production pace, serving both commercial projects and the Foundation’s internal needs, as well as large-scale institutional contracts. Its work extends beyond Braille printing to include technical preparation, specialized proofreading, and quality control, ensuring that every publication reaches readers with full accessibility. The continued growth of Braille and accessible digital production reflects Fundação Dorina’s commitment to democratizing access to knowledge, keeping pace with technological advances while expanding opportunities for inclusion for increasingly diverse audiences.

Throughout 2025, despite ongoing renovations at the Foundation’s headquarters and operational challenges, the Editorial and Printing areas maintained a high level of productivity, demonstrating the institution’s ability to adapt, innovate, and continue expanding its reach without interrupting services to the population.

São Paulo Tax Receipt Program (Nota Fiscal Paulista – NFP)

 

The São Paulo Tax Receipt Program (Nota Fiscal Paulista – NFP) is one of Fundação Dorina’s key fundraising initiatives. Through partnerships with retail businesses, collection boxes are made available for customers to donate tax receipts issued without a taxpayer identification number (CPF). These receipts can then be allocated to the Foundation and converted into funding that supports programs promoting inclusion and accessibility for visually impaired people.

In 2025, the campaign engaged 33 volunteers, who entered 1,385,633 tax receipts into the system over the course of the year, generating R$ 404,941.64 in funding. The continued success of this initiative highlights the importance of public participation and partnerships with the retail sector in securing resources that directly support the maintenance and expansion of the Foundation’s services.

São Paulo Municipal Council for Social Assistance (COMAS/SP)

 

Through its Advocacy area, Fundação Dorina represented the social assistance sector on the São Paulo Municipal Council for Social Assistance (COMAS/SP), working to protect and advance social assistance rights, particularly those of people with visual impairments. Throughout the year, the Foundation’s participation contributed to strengthening the Council’s transparency, governance, and administrative processes, generating tangible benefits for civil society organizations.

In 2025, implementation continued under COMAS Resolution No. 2118/2023, which regulates the registration and renewal processes for social assistance organizations. In response to a backlog of applications from previous years, a dedicated task force was established to review and regularize pending cases. As a result, approximately 536 registration and renewal applications were assessed, accelerating procedures while introducing clearer and less bureaucratic processes.

The Advocacy team also contributed to the development of the COMAS Practical Guide for the Registration and Renewal of Organizations, created to enhance transparency and facilitate access to the information organizations need to maintain compliance with the Council’s requirements.

Another major achievement was participation in drafting and approving the COMAS Code of Ethics, an initiative designed to strengthen governance, integrity, and institutional accountability.

Throughout the year, the Foundation also served on the Conference Organizing Committee, responsible for coordinating the 32 Regional Social Assistance Conferences and the 16th Municipal Social Assistance Conference, which brought together approximately 9,000 participants. This work included technical meetings, contract oversight, coordination with the Municipal Secretariat for Social Assistance, collaboration with regional supervisory offices, and on-site support during the conferences.

In the area of public policy financing, the Council approved a record volume of funding through parliamentary amendments, allocating more than BRL 18 million to social assistance organizations across the municipality. It also approved a resolution presenting, for the first time, a detailed breakdown of the 2026 social assistance budget, estimated at BRL 5.2 billion. Additionally, COMAS reviewed and approved the 2024 financial and programmatic accountability report submitted by the Municipal Secretariat for Social Assistance, reinforcing the Council’s statutory oversight responsibilities and promoting greater transparency in public administration.

Permanent Accessibility Commission (CPA)

Paranapiacaba: Advancing Accessibility in a Historic Landmark of São Paulo

 

In 2025, as part of its participation in the State Fund for the Protection of Diffuse Interests (FID), Fundação Dorina’s Advocacy area took part in the inauguration of the renovation of the Pedra Lisa Trail, located in the historic village of Paranapiacaba, in the municipality of Santo André.

Renowned for its close connection to the historic São Paulo Railway, Paranapiacaba preserves buildings and landscapes dating back to the expansion of the railway network in the State of São Paulo. Today, it is recognized as an important site of historical, cultural, and environmental heritage. The project was officially inaugurated at Cine Lyra, regarded as the first movie theater in the State of São Paulo, originally built to serve the workers of the British-owned railway who lived in the region.

Funded through the FID, the project combined infrastructure improvements with accessibility enhancements, expanding access to the site while reinforcing the principle that heritage preservation initiatives should also promote accessibility and ensure everyone’s right to experience cultural and natural heritage.

Fundação Dorina’s involvement in this initiative underscores its commitment to advancing public policies that recognize inclusion as an essential component of sustainable development, helping make historic and tourist destinations increasingly accessible to all.
